Methods

How this was measured.

Every figure on this site rests on a definition, a coding decision and an error bar. This page is all three, written out, including the parts that are inconvenient.

First, the thing that matters most

This site is a prototype and every number on it is simulated. The figures are populated by a seeded random generator so that the interface could be designed, built and tested before the analysis was finished. The outlet names, party names, leader names, account handles, headlines and AI answers are all invented.

They are invented deliberately rather than borrowed from real organisations: a prototype that attached fabricated bias scores to real newsrooms would be misleading the moment a screenshot left this page. What is real is everything below — the measures, the coding schemes, the uncertainty treatment and the limits. When the analysis lands, the data is swapped in and the pages stay as they are.

The two campaigns

The study covers two simultaneous campaigns: a provincial general election in Quebec and a referendum in Alberta, each over a 41-day campaign period. Eight news sources are sampled across both, chosen to span outlet type rather than to be representative of any market: a public broadcaster, two legacy broadsheets, two tabloids, a digital-native outlet, a wire service and a partisan digital outlet.

What each measure means

Visibility
Mentions of an actor per 1,000 articles, and that actor’s share of all actor mentions on a given day. A mention is a named reference resolved to a canonical entity. It counts presence, never approval.
Polarisation / dispersion
The standard deviation, across the eight sources, of source-level mean stance toward an actor, issue or position. Stance runs −1 to +1 and is coded per article by a classifier validated against a hand-coded sample. High dispersion means the sources differed from each other — not that any of them was wrong.
Linguistic bias
Six dimensions, scored per article and standardised so that 0 is the corpus mean and units are standard deviations: epistemological framing, intensifiers, attribution gap, agency deletion, distancing, and conflict metaphor. Because scores are relative to this corpus, a source at 0 is average — not neutral.
Visual framing
Five codes applied to published photographs: camera angle, shot size, expression, setting and gaze, plus an image–caption congruence score. Coded by two independent coders on a sample; disagreements adjudicated. This is the least reliable measurement in the study.
Message uptake
The share of a campaign message’s appearances in the actor’s own channels that are echoed in news coverage within 72 hours. Near-identical phrasings are de-duplicated, so a repeated line counts once.
Framing shift
The difference between a claim’s framing valence in the actor’s own documents and its mean framing valence in news coverage. +1 is entirely the claimant’s frame, −1 entirely the counter-frame.
Engagement
Reshares per 1,000 followers, analysed on a log scale. An outcome of platform ranking as much as of content; never interpretable as an effect of the text alone.
Lagged association
The correlation between coverage tone on day t and the change in polling estimate on day t + k, computed for every k from −14 to +14. Reported as the whole curve, never as a single selected lag.
AI assistant stance and hedging
Each of five assistants is prompted 40 times per question per language on a fixed date. Stance and hedging are coded on the returned text with the same schemes used for news. Results are distributions at a moment, not properties of a model.

Uncertainty

Uncertainty is drawn wherever it exists and can be drawn: 95% intervals on polling series, confidence intervals on every lag correlation, and article counts behind every heatmap cell and scatter point so you can see how thin a measurement is before you lean on it.

Where it cannot be drawn honestly — inter-coder disagreement on visual codes, for instance, or the non-determinism of an AI assistant — it is stated in words in the takeaway box at the end of that route. Those boxes are part of the finding, not a legal notice appended to it.

What this study cannot tell you

  • Whether any newsroom is biased. We measure differences between sources on defined dimensions. Differences have many causes — style guides, section conventions, beat structure, deadline pressure, who happened to be free that afternoon — and this design cannot separate them from editorial intent.
  • Whether coverage changed anyone’s vote. Nothing here is an experiment. The strongest claim any figure on this site supports is temporal precedence, and only for one pair of series.
  • Whether any claim discussed is true. We code how claims were framed and how far they travelled. Fact-checking them is a different study.
  • What readers took away. We measure what was published and what was shared. Reception is not observed at all.
  • Whether the patterns generalise. Two simultaneous campaigns in one country is the easiest possible comparison. Treat the transferability results in Path 7 as an upper bound.

Accessibility and how to read the figures

  • Every figure has a Table button that shows the same numbers as text, so no value is reachable only by hovering or only by colour.
  • Series are identified by a legend and, where there are few enough of them, labelled directly on the chart — colour never carries identity on its own.
  • Two measures are never plotted on two y-axes. Where the site needs to show coverage volume against polling, they sit in two stacked panels sharing one time axis.
  • The palette is a validated colourblind-safe categorical set; the light and dark versions are separately chosen rather than inverted. The theme follows your system setting and can be switched in the top bar.
  • Scroll-driven animation respects prefers-reduced-motion; with it on, every figure appears in its final state and the step boxes stay fully legible.

Reproducibility

The placeholder data is generated deterministically from fixed seeds in js/content/_data.js, so the site renders identically on every load. Each generator carries a comment describing the shape it returns; replacing a generator with real results requires no change to the figure code, the narrative or the layout.
