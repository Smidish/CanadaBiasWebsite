# Same Campaign, Different Story
### Concept & content blueprint for the interactive research site

**Subject:** media bias analysis of the Quebec general election and the Alberta referendum
**Format:** scrollytelling + choose-your-own-path explorable, in the spirit of *The Pudding*
**Status of this document:** editorial blueprint. All figures currently run on **simulated
placeholder data** generated by a seeded RNG (`js/content/_data.js`). Swapping in real
results means replacing the data generators — the narrative scaffolding, layout and chart
code stay as they are.

---

## 1. The premise

Two campaigns ran at the same time in two provinces. Millions of words, thousands of
images, uncountable posts, and now a fresh layer: AI assistants answering questions about
both. This project measures how that coverage was built — who appeared, in whose words,
with what framing, and whose messages made it through.

**The reader's role.** The site treats the reader as the analyst, not the audience.
Language is warm and direct ("Pick a source. Watch who disappears."), but every claim is
tied to a measurement, and every measurement is shown with its uncertainty and its
limits. The closing beat of every path is explicitly *not* a verdict: it is a handoff.

> **Editorial rule used throughout:** we describe *what the measurement says* and never
> *what it means you should think*. Words like "biased", "misleading", "unfair" never
> appear in our own voice — we say "more negative in tone", "quoted less often",
> "framed as conflict rather than policy". The reader draws the conclusion.

**Voice, in three lines**
- Second person, present tense, short sentences. "You've just picked a newsroom."
- Numbers get a plain-language twin. "3.4× — for every mention of the No campaign's
  economic case, there were three of the Yes campaign's."
- Never triumphant. The hero framing is a *hint* — "your turn", "you decide what
  matters" — never quests, levels, or badges.

## 2. Site architecture

```
index.html              The Atrium — hero, the seven doors, how to read the site
tour.html               The Guided Tour — the default path, one headline finding per RQ
path.html?rq=1..7       Any of the seven paths (single template, content-driven)
methods.html            How everything is measured, glossary, limits, data availability
```

**Navigation model**

- The **hub** (`index.html`) offers seven doors, each an RQ rewritten as a human question.
- The **default path** (`tour.html`) is a 7-beat guided tour: the single most significant
  result from each RQ, in a deliberate order, each beat ending in "go deeper" into that
  path. A reader who only scrolls and never clicks still gets the whole study.
- Each **path** opens with a *crossroads*: 2–3 routes (sub-analyses). Routes are not
  hidden — they are all on the page — but the crossroads lets the reader jump to the one
  they care about and skip the rest. Progress (paths visited, routes read) persists in
  `localStorage` and is drawn in the top rail.
- Every path ends with **"Where next?"**, offering two thematically linked paths plus the
  tour.

**Page rhythm inside a path**

```
path header       numeral · name · the question in plain language
crossroads        2–3 route cards; "take them in order" is the default
route  (a SCENE — a pinned stage, not a stretch of page)
  ├ title card    name, opening, hero number, over an accent wash and an
  │               ambient canvas that previews the figure's shape
  ├ handover      title clears, then the figure appears in place
  ├ steps         4–6 boxes scroll over the pinned figure — the only thing
  │               on the page that actually moves
  ├ exit          the whole stage fades out while still pinned
  ├ explorer      the same figure, handed over — filters, toggles, hover
  └ takeaway      "What this does and doesn't show" — a stated limit, every time
crossroads-out    where next
```

**Why a scene and not a scroll.** The point of the choose-your-own-path structure is that
each route is a separate thing you decided to look at. If the previous route's explorer and
takeaway are still on screen when the next route's headline arrives, it reads as one long
article with rules between the parts. Pinning each route and fading it out before the next
one begins makes the boundary an event rather than a divider — and it means the figure can
*appear* where it will live, instead of sliding up from under the text.

**Finding your way.** A left-hand contents rail lists the sections of the current page, marks
where you are, fills one pip per step inside the active route, and jumps on click.

## 3. The seven paths

Each path = one RQ. Name, the human question, routes, figures.

---

### Path 1 · **Who Gets the Mic**
*RQ1 — visibility and polarisation of actors, groups, issues and positions across sources*

> **The question you're asking:** In a campaign, attention is the scarcest resource. Who
> received it, who was left out, and did every newsroom make the same call?

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 1A | **The attention market** | `stream` — share of mentions by actor over the campaign, switchable by source; `bars` — final-week ranking |
| 1B | **The polarisation map** | `quadrant` — visibility (x) vs. cross-source disagreement in stance (y), actors and issues plotted together |
| 1C | **Who's missing** | `lollipop` — share of coverage mentions vs. share of population, by social group |

**Step copy, 1A (abridged):** the leaders' share is stable in aggregate → but the
aggregate is an average of newsrooms that disagree → switch to the tabloid and the
third-place party loses a third of its airtime → switch to the public broadcaster and it
comes back → the last week collapses onto two names everywhere.

**Takeaway / limit:** mention counts measure *presence*, not *approval*. An actor can
dominate coverage that is uniformly negative. Path 2 measures tone.

---

### Path 2 · **The Words We Choose**
*RQ2 — linguistic bias and textual/visual framing across sources covering the same thing*

> **The question you're asking:** Two newsrooms, one event, one afternoon. What changes
> between them when the facts don't?

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 2A | **Same event, seven headlines** | `headlines` — pick an event, read every source's headline with the bias-carrying spans annotated on hover |
| 2B | **The loaded-language meter** | `heatmap` — source × bias dimension (epistemological, framing, attribution asymmetry, agency deletion, intensifiers, metaphor) |
| 2C | **What the photographs say** | `imagegrid` — coded stills (angle, shot size, expression, setting) per actor per source; `diverging` — image–caption congruence |

**Step copy, 2A (abridged):** here is the event, stripped to its facts → here are the
seven headlines → three chose the word "insists", four chose "says" → the same verb choice
tracks across 1,400 other articles → and the body text moves the other way from the
headline more often than you'd guess.

**Takeaway / limit:** these are *measurable* differences in wording, not proof of intent.
Style guides, deadline pressure and section conventions all produce the same signatures.

---

### Path 3 · **From Press Release to Print**
*RQ3 — which party / candidate / advocacy messages get covered, and how they change*

> **The question you're asking:** Campaigns push messages. Newsrooms pick some up and
> drop the rest. Follow one message through the pipe.

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 3A | **The message funnel** | `flow` — actor statements → paid advertising → news coverage, ribbon width = volume, colour = originating actor |
| 3B | **The uptake leaderboard** | `bars` — uptake rate per message frame; toggle by party/advocacy org |
| 3C | **Lost in translation** | `slope` — framing of the same claim in the actor's own words vs. in news rendering |

**Takeaway / limit:** uptake is not endorsement. A message can be covered *because* it is
contested. Route 3C measures the direction of reframing, not its fairness.

---

### Path 4 · **The Amplifiers**
*RQ4 — which accounts amplify which positions, how they reframe news, and what engagement follows*

> **The question you're asking:** A story leaves the newsroom and enters the feed. What
> happens to it there?

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 4A | **The amplifier network** | `network` — accounts and outlets as nodes, sharing as edges, communities coloured |
| 4B | **Does bias travel further?** | `scatter` — linguistic bias score of a post vs. engagement, with a fitted trend and a highlighted subset |
| 4C | **The reply room** | `diverging` — stance and sentiment of replies, by post archetype |

**Takeaway / limit:** engagement is an outcome of the platform's ranking as much as of the
text. The trend line is an association measured on observational data — not an effect.

---

### Path 5 · **Coverage and the Polls**
*RQ5 — how topics, visibility, bias and framing move with polls and prediction markets*

> **The question you're asking:** Coverage and standing move together. The interesting
> question is which one moves first.

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 5A | **The race and the coverage** | `timeline` — poll estimates with uncertainty bands, coverage-volume ribbon underneath, campaign events annotated |
| 5B | **Lead or lag?** | `bars` (signed) — cross-correlation of coverage tone against polling change at lags −14…+14 days |
| 5C | **The market's ear** | `timeline` (event-study mode) — prediction-market price around the five largest coverage shocks |

**Takeaway / limit:** the strongest statement this design supports is *temporal
precedence*, not causation. Both series can respond to the same unobserved event.

---

### Path 6 · **Ask the Machine**
*RQ6 — how AI assistants represent competing actors, issues and positions*

> **The question you're asking:** A voter asks an assistant who to believe. Five
> assistants answer. Do they answer the same way — in both languages, in both months?

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 6A | **Five models, one question** | `models` — pick a campaign question, read each assistant's answer with stance and hedging coded inline |
| 6B | **Does the language change the answer?** | `slope` — stance per model, English vs. French, same question |
| 6C | **Who do they cite?** | `bars` — citation share by outlet, against that outlet's share of the actual published corpus |

**Takeaway / limit:** assistants are non-deterministic and are updated without notice.
Every point is a distribution over repeated prompts at a stated date, not a fixed property
of a model.

---

### Path 7 · **Two Campaigns, One Pattern**
*RQ7 — what generalises across the Quebec election and the Alberta referendum, and what doesn't*

> **The question you're asking:** One province chose a government, the other answered a
> question. Strip away the subject matter — is the coverage built the same way?

| Route | Name | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 7A | **The shared playbook** | `lollipop` (dumbbell) — effect size of each pattern in Quebec vs. Alberta, sorted by gap |
| 7B | **Fingerprints** | `radar` — each event's profile across eight coverage dimensions, overlaid |
| 7C | **What transfers** | `heatmap` — a model trained on one event, scored on the other, per measure |

**Takeaway / limit:** an election and a referendum differ in structure — many actors
versus two camps. Some gaps are about the contest, not the coverage.

## 4. The default path (`tour.html`)

Seven beats, one per RQ, ordered as a story rather than by number:

1. **Attention is finite** (RQ1) — the final-week collapse onto two names.
2. **The same event, different verbs** (RQ2) — the "says / insists / claims" split.
3. **What the pictures did** (RQ2, visual) — the low-angle / high-angle asymmetry.
4. **The message that didn't make it** (RQ3) — highest-spend message, lowest uptake.
5. **The feed reframes** (RQ4) — what changes between headline and share text.
6. **Coverage moved first** (RQ5) — the lag result, stated carefully.
7. **And the machines?** (RQ6) — the EN/FR divergence, then RQ7 as the closing frame.

Each beat is a single sticky figure with 3 steps and a "go deeper" door into its path.

## 5. Figure library

Fifteen reusable renderers cover every figure above. Each takes `(container, spec, state)`
and re-renders on scroll steps and explorer controls.

| id | Form | Data job | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stream` | stacked area over time | composition over time | 1A, 5A |
| `quadrant` | scatter with quadrant guides | two-measure positioning | 1B |
| `lollipop` | paired dot / dumbbell | comparison of two values per row | 1C, 7A |
| `heatmap` | matrix of cells | magnitude across two categories | 2B, 7C |
| `bars` | ranked horizontal bars, signed-capable | magnitude & ranking | 1A, 3B, 5B, 6C |
| `flow` | three-stage ribbon diagram | flow / attrition | 3A |
| `slope` | two-position slope chart | change between two conditions | 3C, 6B |
| `network` | force-directed graph | relational structure | 4A |
| `scatter` | x/y points + fitted trend | association | 4B |
| `diverging` | diverging stacked bars | polarity of a distribution | 2C, 4C |
| `timeline` | multi-line + band + annotations | change over time, events | 5A, 5C |
| `radar` | radial profile | multi-dimensional profile comparison | 7B |
| `headlines` | annotated HTML text | qualitative close reading | 2A |
| `imagegrid` | coded tile grid | visual-content coding | 2C |
| `models` | answer cards with inline coding | qualitative model comparison | 6A |

**Design constraints applied to all of them** (from the data-viz method):
- One y-axis per plot. Never two scales. Where two measures must be compared they are
  indexed to a common base or split into two plots.
- Colour follows the entity, never its rank. Filtering never repaints the survivors.
- Categorical hues in fixed slot order, capped at 8; scatter/all-pairs forms cap at 3
  hues and otherwise use emphasis (highlight one, grey the rest).
- Sequential = one hue light→dark; diverging = blue↔red with a neutral grey midpoint.
- Every figure has a hover layer and a **table view** twin, so no value is reachable only
  by colour or only by tooltip.
- Palette validated with the skill's validator against this site's own surfaces
  (`#faf9f5` light, `#191917` dark) — all checks pass in both modes.

## 6. Data model (placeholder)

All names below are **fictional placeholders**, defined once in `js/content/_entities.js`
so real outlet, party and account names can be substituted in a single edit.

**Sources (8).** `Radio Boréal` (public broadcaster) · `La Dépêche` (legacy broadsheet, FR)
· `Prairie Herald` (legacy broadsheet, EN) · `Le Matin Express` (tabloid, FR) ·
`The Foothills Post` (tabloid, EN) · `WestWire` (digital native) · `Fil Public` (wire
service) · `Signal` (partisan digital).

**Quebec actors (5 parties + leaders).** Coalition Nationale · Parti Libéral-Démocrate ·
Mouvement Solidaire · Alliance Verte · Parti de la Relance.

**Alberta actors.** Yes camp (`Alberta Forward`), No camp (`One Canada Alberta`), plus
four third-party advocacy organisations.

**Issues (8 per event), social groups (9), linguistic-bias dimensions (6), visual codes
(5), AI assistants (5, anonymised A–E), languages (EN/FR).**

**Measures.**
- *Visibility*: mentions per 1,000 articles, share of mentions, share of quoted speech.
- *Polarisation*: cross-source standard deviation of stance (−1…+1) for an actor/issue.
- *Linguistic bias*: per-dimension score, standardised, 0 = corpus mean.
- *Framing*: proportion of articles carrying each frame; visual codes as proportions.
- *Uptake*: share of an actor's messages appearing in coverage within 72 h.
- *Engagement*: reshares per 1,000 followers (log scale).
- *Association with polling*: cross-correlation coefficient at lag *k* days.

**Dummy-data generation.** `js/content/_data.js` holds a seeded PRNG (mulberry32) plus
helpers for smooth random walks, Dirichlet-ish compositions and correlated pairs, so every
figure is deterministic across reloads and looks organically noisy rather than synthetic.
Each generated series is tagged `simulated: true`; the UI shows a persistent
**"Simulated data"** badge and every figure caption repeats it.

---

## 7. Responsible-presentation checklist

1. Persistent "simulated data — placeholder results" badge in the top rail and on every
   figure caption; it cannot be dismissed.
2. Entity names are fictional and labelled as such, so no real outlet, party or person is
   attached to an invented bias score.
3. Every route ends with a stated limit before the reader moves on.
4. `methods.html` carries the measurement definitions, the uncertainty treatment, and an
   explicit "what this study cannot tell you" section.
5. Associational language everywhere; causal language only where the design supports it
   (it does not, anywhere in the current figure set).

---

## 8. Build notes

- No build step, no framework, no network dependency. D3 v7 is vendored at
  `js/lib/d3.v7.min.js`; classic `<script>` tags only, so the site runs from `file://`
  as well as from a server.
- `js/scrolly.js` — IntersectionObserver step engine (sticky figure + steps, reduced-motion
  aware). `js/charts.js` — the figure library. `js/render.js` — turns a content object into
  a page. `js/app.js` — rail, progress, theme, routing.
- Content lives in `js/content/rq1.js` … `rq7.js` + `tour.js`; each exports a plain object
  on `window.CONTENT`. Copy and data sit side by side so an editor can change both in one
  place.
